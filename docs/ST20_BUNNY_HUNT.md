## 1. The brief

> *"Three arrivals halls, one boulevard of hotels, and a ledger that hops."*

**Interpretation used (recorded so the writer never has to guess):** the ring
moves people by moving their **paper selves** ahead of their bodies. The asset
codenamed **BUNNY** is the reservation ledger of that service — a round-robin
book that hops courier-to-courier through the world's arrival halls, never
sleeping twice under one roof. The crew hunts it across three nodes: **London
Heathrow**, **Guangzhou**, and the **boulevard hotels of Batumi** — where ST-16's
Quiet Hotel keeps a lost-property register that is, quietly, the most honest
book in Georgia.

**Pitch line:** *"Every bunny has a den. Ours has a concierge."*

## 3. New systems

- **LOST PROPERTY evidence class** — the seventh codex class; registers and claim
  tickets, combinable with ROTA (ST-19) for schedule-proof.
- **DECOY ROUND-ROBIN** — N identical carriers, all real; the tail mechanic's
  final form. Reuses M19's pursuit grid with zero new movement code.
- **LOBBY CIRCUIT** — social-stealth reads with a night clock; the campaign's
  first *hospitality* terrain set.
- **Carousel rounds** — a public-space reshuffle that moves neutral units only;
  terrain stays put, crowds rotate. One array shuffle per two rounds.

## 4. Content handling

- Airport chapters contain **no security subject matter at all** (§0-2): the
  word "checkpoint" does not appear; every scene is arrivals-hall public space,
  and the stylised maps match no real terminal.
- The Golden Deer's muscle is non-lethally routed, M19 scoring; civilians are
  never targets and never shields — crowds are terrain, not stakes.
- The night porters are written with ST-7's dignity convention: workers with a
  quiet union of their own, never played for pity, never shown harmed. Their
  protection guarantee is the thread's win condition, not a reward.
- Georgian continuity passes through the same native-review gate (G3) as ST-16;
  hotel names invented, boulevard stylised.
